# Deck recipes (slide skeletons). Titles are takeaways; the ones below show the shape, not the words.

Slide count = talk minutes / 1.5 to 2. Cut before you shrink.

## Hackathon / demo-day pitch (5 to 7 slides)
1. `title`: the promise in one line
2. `statement`: the problem, who feels it, in one sentence
3. `split`: the solution, with a callout for why it's different
4. `steps` or `compare`: how it works / versus the status quo
5. `stat`: the proof (real numbers only: from the ledger)
6. `chart` (optional): the result that mattered
7. `closing`: what's next / the ask

## Research talk or proposal (8 to 12 slides)
`title` > `statement` (the gap) > `split` (hypothesis/approach) > `steps` (method) > `compare` (baseline vs ours) > `chart` x1 or 2 (results, one claim each) > `stat` (headline numbers) > `statement` (limitations, honestly) > `closing` (next experiments, what you'd need)
Put the main claim in slide 2 or 3, not at the end. Cite sources in a `source` line or notes.

## Project walkthrough (6 to 9 slides)
`title` > `statement` (what it does and why) > `steps` (architecture as a flow) > `split` (key design decision + callout) > `stat` (scale/performance, real) > `compare` (before/after) > `closing` (what you'd build next)

## Short intro / placement deck (4 to 6 slides)
`title` > `statement` (what you do, one sentence) > `stat` (3 proof points) > `split` (best project, with a callout for the outcome) > `closing` (what you want next + contact)

## Results-heavy update (6 to 10 slides)
`title` > `statement` (the headline finding) > `chart` per finding (title states the finding) > `stat` > `compare` > `closing` (decision needed)

## Pre-read versus live
A live deck uses few words and big visuals. A pre-read carries the whole story, so titles + notes do more work. Say which you built.
